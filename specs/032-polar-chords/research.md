# Research: Polar Chords

No `NEEDS CLARIFICATION` remains in the spec; the user confirmed the assumptions of the spec on 2026-11-06 ("assumptions sound good"). The questions below are about how to build it.

## Decision 1: The bars are worked out first, apart from the drawing

- **Decision**: A new module `backend/app/services/polar_chords.py` with `polar_bars(smoothed, beat, count)`, returning for the whole file small arrays: `note_slots` `(frames, count)` (arc numbers 0 to 83, -1 for no bar), `note_shares` `(frames, count)` (0 to 1), `chord_place` `(frames,)` (0 to 83, -1 for none; 0 to 6 until the change of 2026-11-07), `chord_share` `(frames,)` and `length` `(frames,)` (`0.5 + 0.5 × position`). `write_polar_frames(bars, colormap, directory, width, height)` draws them.
- **Rationale**: As for Decay, the numbers and the drawing are two jobs; the first is testable as plain arrays (the places, the ties, the lengths), the second at the pixel level. The bars of all frames are independent, so drawing is a loop over frames with no state.
- **Alternatives considered**: working the bars out inside the drawing loop (harder to test); one array of 91 values per frame (84 notes + 7 chord places, mostly zeros: simple, but the bars are 9 numbers a frame).

## Decision 2: The notes of a frame

- **Decision**: The `N` loudest of the 84 drawn notes (columns 0 to 83 of the smoothed values), by a stable sort on the negative strength so that ties go to the lower note, keeping only those with strength above 0. Strength share `s = sqrt(value / peak)`, `peak` the largest value of the whole smoothed array (all 88 columns, as the tile colors use); 0 when the peak is 0.
- **Rationale**: The same rules as Decay's five loudest notes and the tile colors (features 027 and 010), so that the notes mean the same in every picture.
- **Alternatives considered**: ranking relative to the loudest note of the frame (Concentric's rule: every frame would have a full-strength bar however quiet it is); the lowest `N` notes (not what the spec says).

## Decision 3: The chord of a frame, and its place

- **Decision**: `chords.measure_chords(smoothed, POLAR_CHORD_TYPES)` once, with `POLAR_CHORD_TYPES = ((7,), (4, 7), (3, 7), (4, 10), (4, 11), (3, 7, 10), (4, 6))` and no limits (`note_count` and `threshold` not given: the image type has neither setting). **The place of a chord is the index of its type** (0 to 6): the chord ring has 7 arcs, one for each type, and no root. The chord strength share is `sqrt(mean(value of the record's notes) / peak)`. (Revised 2026-11-06: the first version also worked out the root letter from the recorded notes and the inversion, `root = (notes[0] − (0, *type)[inversion]) mod 12`, and had 84 places, `type_index × 12 + root`; the user asked for the type only.)
- **Rationale**: Feature 031 already records exactly one chord per window by the rules the user wrote, so this image type only places it. The type index of the series is the place, no further calculation is needed, and the inversion, the octave and the root cannot change it. For a fourth, `(7)` is recorded in first inversion, which is the same bar as a fifth.
- **Alternatives considered**: all of the 7 types that fit, each with a bar (would need a different measurement; the user's rules choose one); 7 types × 12 roots = 84 places (the first version; replaced at the user's request).

## Decision 4: Drawing with arcs, per-size geometry kept, only the bars touched

- **Decision**: `_geometry(width, height)`, cached for the frame size, holds for each pixel inside the full radius plus a pixel: its distance `r` from the center `(width / 2, height / 2)`, its clockwise angle from the top `θ = atan2(dx, −dy)` (with `dx` to the right and `dy` down, in `[0, 2π)`), its note arc `⌊θ ÷ Δ⌋` and its chord arc `⌊(2π − θ) ÷ Δ⌋ mod 84` (`Δ = 2π ÷ 84`), and for each of the two kinds the neighbouring arc and its share of the pixel `w = clip(0.5 − d, 0, 1)` with `d` the distance in pixels to the nearer arc edge, `min(f, 1 − f) × Δ × r`, `f` the fraction of the arc already passed. For each arc, the pixel indices where it is the own arc (weight `1 − w`) and where it is the neighbour (weight `w`) are kept. A bar of length `L` pixels has coverage `clip(L − r + 0.5, 0, 1)` on those pixels. A frame builds an alpha and a premultiplied color array only on the pixels of its bars, first for the note bars, composited over the background (the colormap's middle color, row 128), then the chord bar over that.
- **Rationale**: Only 9 bars of at most 1,400 pixels each are touched per frame, so a frame costs about what the PNG costs. The edge smoothing is the way the rings of Concentric are smoothed (half a pixel on each side), which keeps the look of the round pictures without supersampling (4 × 4 supersampling of 240,000 pixels per frame would be slower than the budget allows).
- **Alternatives considered**: supersampling like Circles (about 16 times the pixels per frame); a polygon drawing library (a new dependency); computing the angle per frame (the same numbers every time).

## Decision 5: Colors and lengths

- **Decision**: Length in pixels `L = (0.5 + 0.5 × p) × R`, `p` the beat position of the frame from `check_beat` (0 when there is no beat), `R = min(width, height) / 2 − 10` (190 on 600 × 400). Colors use the colormap table (`lookup_table`, 256 rows): notes row `rint((0.5 − 0.5 × s) × 255)`, the chord row `rint((0.5 + 0.5 × s) × 255)`.
- **Rationale**: Exactly the spec's formulas; the rounding to a table row is the way every picture colors.
- **Alternatives considered**: interpolating between table rows (no other picture does).

## Decision 6: Wiring, the page and the setting

- **Decision**: `IMAGE_TYPES` gets `"polar-chords"`; `polar_chords.check_polar_notes(value)` returns an `int` for a whole number from 5 to 8 (booleans, fractions, strings refused; message `The number of notes shown must be a whole number from 5 to 8.`); `write_frames(..., polar_notes=DEFAULT_POLAR_NOTES)` checks it for every type and draws Polar Chords after Decay Exp from the smoothed values and the beat; `jobs.py` parses the optional text field `polar_notes` (not sent: 5; anything sent must be valid), measures the beat when the type is `polar-chords` as for `concentric`, and echoes `polar_notes` for every type. The page: `isPolarType` for the grid settings (do not apply), the note layout (applies: it says in which arc each note is) and the Local peaks (apply, no checkbox); a type group `polar-chords` with the one control `polar-notes`; the summary shows "Notes shown" for the type only.
- **Rationale**: The pattern of Decay's rate (checked for every type, used by one), so that the existing handling of settings, echoes and tests carries over. Polar Chords is not one of the "rings types" (`isRingsType`), which would also have the grid settings behave as they do for circles; it has its own `isPolarType`, and `noteLayoutApplies` includes it.
- **Alternatives considered**: adding the type to `isRingsType` (the rings types have a note layout too, but would have shared other behaviour with circles that Polar Chords does not need); a fixed number of notes (the spec has the setting).

## Decision 7: The note layout is the arc order (added 2026-11-06)

- **Decision**: `polar_bars(..., note_layout="rows")` takes the layout name (checked with `note_layout.check_note_layout`, the message of the other pictures). The layout's `note_order(layout)` lists the note drawn in each cell, so the arc of a note is its cell: `arc_of_note = argsort(note_order(layout))` (the inverse permutation). A note's slot is `arc_of_note[note]`. The ranking of the loudest notes stays by note (a stable sort, ties to the lower note) and the chord is measured on the unarranged values, so only `note_slots` change. For `rows` the order is the identity and the pictures are byte-identical to before.
- **Rationale**: One line of difference, the same layout code as Tiles, Concentric and Decay, and the same request field `note_layout`, which is already parsed, checked, passed to `write_frames` and echoed for every image type. On the page the layout group is shown by `noteLayoutApplies`.
- **Alternatives considered**: arranging the values with `arrange_notes` and ranking in cell order (ties would then go to the lower cell, not the lower note, and the chord would have to be measured on the unarranged values anyway); a different arrangement for the arcs (the user asked for the existing two).

## Decision 8: The note bars follow their notes (added 2026-11-06)

- **Decision**: `note_targets` gives, for every frame and every volume (rank by loudness), the arc of the note and its share, as before. `follow_arcs(targets)` then keeps one arc per volume: it starts in the arc of the note (first frame, or after a frame without a note for the volume), and each frame moves one arc toward the target: `d = (target − current) mod 84`; `d = 0` stays, `d ≤ 42` goes `+1`, otherwise `−1`. A loop over the frames (thousands, with 8 volumes at a time in numpy) is all it takes. `polar_bars` hides, in each frame, a bar whose arc equals the arc of a volume with a lower number (a louder one); the tracking itself is not changed by hiding.
- **Rationale**: One arc per volume is what the user described ("keep track of the angle of each volume") and what the Circles picture does (feature 019: a circle is placed relative to the circle of the same rank in the previous frame). The shorter way round is the natural reading of "in the direction of the correct note" on a circle, with a fixed choice (clockwise) for the one case in which both ways are equal. Hiding the covered bar keeps the rule that the arcs of the bars of a frame are all different, so the smooth edges between neighbouring bars (which add up the coverage of two bars next to each other) stay exact; two bars in the same arc would otherwise add up to more than the full coverage and the colors would be wrong.
- **Alternatives considered**: one bar for each note instead of each volume (a note that becomes silent would fade or slide away, and many more bars; not what the user described); moving by a share of the distance (a bar would slow down near its note and never arrive exactly); a speed in arcs per second (depends on the frame rate in a different way; the user said one note per move); drawing the covered bar under the louder one (needs a different way of adding the coverage); keeping a silent volume's place (a bar would slide in from an old place after a rest).
